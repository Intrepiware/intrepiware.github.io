Inspired by [Rolling Stone's 200 Greatest Rock Songs of the 21st Century (So Far)](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/best-rock-songs-of-the-21st-century-1235634033/), I decided to create my own century's best songs list.

After reading [Part 1](/music/2026/11/02/best-songs-of-the-21st-century-any-genre/), I'd forgive you for thinking this list would be 25 mainstream pop hits. Pop is great, and those particular songs struck a chord with me, but note they are at the _bottom_ of my list. This installment starts to pivot into my preferred music taste: heartfelt songs with emotion, energy, and vulnerability.

---

### No 20: Samia - Honey (2023)

In a letter sent out with pre-ordered vinyls of Samia’s album Honey she wrote, “[The song is] about a time in my life where I was drinking every day to distract myself from the pain...” This song perfectly captures escapism: mentally vacationing elsewhere to endure the hard unavoidable circumstances one lives through. Repeating "it's all honey, it's all honey" as self-reassurance that _everything is fine_.

Supposedly, Samia originally wrote an emotional version of this song and gave it to a friend to "rework"; he came back with this happy clap-clap campfire singalong of which Samia doesn't entirely approve. (I suspect her strained expression around 2:00 in the video is a reaction to this song's reception)

Choosing only one Samia song for this list feels criminal, she's an _outstanding_ songwriter. Go listen to [Kill Her Freak Out](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dENo8nmvzN8) or [Pool](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IIh7eud8m70) if you're unfamiliar with her work.

### No 19: Eminem - Lose Yourself (2002)

This song transcends cliche; you've heard it 100,000 times, but it still sounds as good as the first listening. Empowering, self-motivating, confidence inspiring, Eminem perfectly captures those high-stakes do-or-die situations we've all been through.

### No 18: Man/Woman/Chainsaw - Nosedive (2026)

An instant classic (a song from this year!) The musicality of this song hits the mark for me. It begins with a simple electronic three-note beat, but adds layers upon layers as the song builds. Beautiful vocal harmonization, drums, guitar, and double-stop violins wander in until climaxing with a complex wall-of-sound finale.

There is only one other song where, first hearing it, I literally stopped what I was doing to fully absorb it (the other was [Elite](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BDeGSXVDihk), if you are wondering).

### No 17: Interpol - Evil (2004)

Dark and mysterious with a catchy bass line and rapid-fire, patter singing. This song is believed to be about [Fred and Rosemary West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_West).

### No 16: The Eels - Hey Man (2005)

Perhaps this song is a little _too_ on the nose (or rips off a [certain 1980s theme song](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WmgIXH4Bp9g)). Contrasting the experience of a beautiful sunrise to the feeling of unrequited love, Mr. E builds the case that life is a combination of the euphoric and miserable, incomplete without either one.
