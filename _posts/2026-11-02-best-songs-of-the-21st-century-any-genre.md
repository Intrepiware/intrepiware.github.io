Inspired by [Rolling Stone's 200 Greatest Rock Songs of the 21st Century (So Far)](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/best-rock-songs-of-the-21st-century-1235634033/), I decided to make my own century's best songs list.

Assembling this list was fun. I made a list of 25 songs, then broke through the analysis paralysis by using an [ELO Ranker](https://wihanza.com/elo-rank/) to sort them.

My list only has 25 songs because they were the only ones I really enjoyed enough to rank (and frankly, Rolling Stone's list has a lot of filler). Full disclosure: I also have a huge blind spot between 2013 and 2024, when I didn't listen to much music. Finally, I liked the format of Rolling Stone's list, so I stole it.

---

### No 25: Mark Ronson ft. Bruno Mars - Uptown Funk (2014)

This song seemed to come from nowhere. When it burst on the scene in 2014, it immediately became a cultural phenomenon. I've never seen a song where the featured artist upstaged the primary artist quite like this one (though I'm sure cashing seven-figure royalty checks helps Mark numb the pain).

### No 24: A Perfect Circle - The Package (2003)

A highly descriptive song about the emotional roller coaster of buying drugs. Sometimes the procurement is as exhilirating as the substances themselves. A friend of mine argued strongly that this song is actually about a man trying to pick up women. But the album is named the "Thirteenth Step" (as in the twelve steps of recovery), so I stand by my interpretation.

Bonus: this song sounds _incredible_ on a good pair of headphones.

### No 21: Atmosphere - The Best Day (2010)

Half gallows humor, half group therapy session. This song verbalizes those times we feel like some higher power is picking on us in particular. Listing relatable grievences like incompetent managers, class warfare at school, and endless overdraft notices, Atmosphere somehow turns a catalog of depressing events into a pep rally. But it's the end of the song, where they remind us, "you're not alone, it's hard as hell ... we'll be right here with you through your war," where we really feel seen.
